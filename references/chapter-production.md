# Multi-section talks and long-form explanations

Read the requested scope and substantive source sections before choosing the opening. For a research presentation, map the problem, contribution, method, results and implications; for course material, map the requested chapter. Match the coverage to what the speaker has asked to explain.

Use the [design conversation](creative-first.md#design-the-explanation) to agree on the central objects, evidence or examples and their simplifications before production. A coverage table is not a substitute for that agreement. Reuse prior answers about style, but settle content choices that the new material introduces.

## Coverage without slide copying

Keep a compact source-to-sequence table: source locator, claim or concept, supporting evidence, visual action, and depth (worked / explained / deferred). Group repeated material into one reasoning sequence; split a dense section if needed. Preserve the dependencies of the argument and disclose substantive omissions or unresolved source errors.

Estimate duration from conceptual load. Reuse an agreed duration; otherwise state a working budget and proceed if it does not materially narrow the task. If the time limit cannot cover the scope, ask about that tradeoff rather than silently dropping the latter half.

## Continuity across sections

Follow the agreed central method, system or example while useful. Carry node names, colors, reference directions and quantities forward. A small starter may isolate an operation; reapply it to the main example so the audience sees its interactions. Retain or re-establish the next argument's required state. Use a separate example where the main one cannot exhibit coupling or degeneracy. A simpler example that removes an agreed structural feature is a design change to discuss, not a layout optimization.

Use native scene classes at useful revision boundaries, not one class per source page. Keep an ordered list of classes/clips in rebuild notes. Render into a fresh build directory; concatenate compatible silent clips and verify the continuous output, not only the clips.

## Proportionate review

Inspect one demanding sequence early. At completion inspect each section's meaningful state plus crowded transitions. Check numerical claims independently, inspect actual size/fps/duration and fully decode the output. A coverage table documents scope, not explanatory clarity. Give humans the continuous video and a few specific review questions, not a pile of compulsory audit paperwork.
